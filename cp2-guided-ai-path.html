CP2 Guided AI Path

About This Path

In this version of CP2, you will use AI for a specific, limited purpose: helping you locate a discourse community and identify possible vocabulary that might matter to your project.

AI is good at producing possibilities.

Your job is to determine whether those possibilities are real, accurate, and actually useful.

Step 1: Revisit Your Direction

Start with your CP1:

  • topic;
  • artifact; and
  • current research question.

Before going any farther, ask yourself whether you still want to spend the semester with them.

If you have realized that your topic is not interesting enough, your artifact does not actually give you much to work with, or another direction has become more compelling, change it now.

This is the last point in the project where a major change is relatively easy.

If you change your topic or artifact, identify the new one at the beginning of your CP2 submission.

Step 2: Identify a Discourse Community

A discourse community is not simply “people interested in my topic.”

It is a group whose members communicate about an area using shared knowledge, practices, conventions, and specialized language.

Depending on your project, that might be a professional field, academic discipline, industry, organized community, hobby community, fan community, artistic community, or another identifiable group.

Give the AI enough information to help you think:

  • your topic;
  • your artifact;
  • your current research question; and
  • what you think you may want to investigate.

You can ask it to suggest discourse communities that might be relevant and explain why.

You make the final choice.

Then write one paragraph identifying the discourse community you have chosen and explaining how it connects to your developing project.

Step 3: Ask AI for Candidate Vocabulary

Next, use AI to help you generate possible specialized terms used by that community.

The important word is possible.

An AI-generated vocabulary list is not research evidence. AI can invent terminology, flatten distinctions, use a real term incorrectly, or suggest words that are technically related to your topic but useless for your particular project.

You are collecting candidates to investigate.

Step 4: Move Beyond the Obvious

Your vocabulary should become more useful as you go.

Do not fill the assignment with terms simply because they are associated with your subject.

Ask:

Will understanding this term actually help me investigate my question, artifact, community, evidence, or eventual argument?

For example, imagine your topic involves how ballet dancers are treated by their companies or dance troupes.

Terms distinguishing pointe shoes from flat shoes might be legitimate ballet vocabulary.

But if your developing question is about emotional pressure, hierarchy, or workplace treatment, shoe terminology may do almost nothing for your research.

It becomes more useful if your project also examines something like:

  • injury;
  • physical expectations;
  • bodily discipline;
  • training practices; or
  • how equipment affects dancers’ bodies.

The term is not good simply because it belongs to the field.

It is good when it helps your project think more precisely.

Your list should therefore move beyond the first obvious words an AI gives you.

Step 5: Verify Every Term

This is the step that makes the AI path research rather than prompt-and-paste.

For every term, find a non-AI source showing that the term is genuinely used in the discourse community.

Possible verification sources include:

  • scholarly articles;
  • professional organizations;
  • disciplinary glossaries;
  • textbooks or reference works;
  • industry publications;
  • reputable community resources;
  • course readings;
  • or another source where the community actually uses or defines the term.

Another AI does not count as verification.

The same AI repeating itself does not count as verification.

If you cannot find evidence that a term is genuinely used, replace it.

Step 6: Define the Terms Yourself

For each of your 10–15 terms, include:

  1. The term
  2. Your definition in your own words
  3. The AI tool that suggested it
  4. The non-AI source where you verified it

Do not paste the source’s definition.

Do not ask AI to rewrite the definition for you.

Read enough to understand the term, then explain what it means yourself.

Step 7: Update Your Research Question

End with the current version of your research question.

It should show what you learned from investigating the discourse community and its vocabulary.

That does not mean you have to jam several new vocabulary terms into the sentence.

Use a term if it makes your question more accurate or precise.

If ordinary language asks the better question, use ordinary language.

The important thing is that your question has developed because your understanding has developed.

What Good Work Looks Like

Strong CP2 work:

  • identifies a specific, defensible discourse community;
  • finds vocabulary actually used by that community;
  • moves beyond obvious or merely topical words;
  • selects terms because they are useful to the project;
  • independently verifies every AI-generated candidate;
  • defines terms in the student’s own language; and
  • ends with a research question that reflects what the student now understands.

AI is helping you find possibilities.

You are doing the research needed to decide which possibilities deserve to stay.
